# Vehicles in Crystalpunk
*Source: Crystalpunk Campaign Guide p. 209* 

Vehicles are commonplace in the world of Crystalpunk, especially in the city of Prismatic Falls. They come in both land and air varieties; and some models of land vehicles hover slightly above the ground. They are all powered by Infernal Engines, potent devices that siphon energy out of one or more captured fiends, and into the vehicle. While these fiends provide a source of fuel for the vehicle, they also require a new soul every few years to be maintained.

Driving these vehicles is a magical process—arcane crystals within the wheel draw mana and information from the user and feed it to the rest of the vehicle to make it operate. As such, it is fairly intuitive, and most people who live in the cities of Aysta know how to drive a vehicle. However, this doesn't mean they drive them competently. Only by having proficiency in a specific vehicle type is a character adept at handling the vehicle, especially in tough situations.

## Vehicle Rules

Here are the rules to implement when using vehicles.

### The Driver

As the driver, the vehicle moves on your initiative, though it should be treated as the vehicle making its move gradually throughout the entire round so as for it to move at a constant rate. You have complete control over the vehicle's speed on your turn, up to its maximum. The vehicle itself cannot Dash or otherwise take actions. On your turn, you must keep at least one hand on the wheel to continue driving. You can otherwise use your action, bonus action, and reaction as normal.

If you wish to perform more complex maneuvers with your vehicle, such as fast turns, jumps, or sideswipes, you must use your action and have two hands on the wheel. When doing such maneuvers, you must make a Dexterity check, adding your proficiency bonus if you are proficient in the type of vehicle you are driving. The DC and outcome is determined by your GM. You might also have to perform such a check when trying to chase or get away from another vehicle, or as a reaction when your car is put into a dangerous situation to avoid losing control or wiping out.

If you attempt to damage another vehicle through a maneuver, make the check against the opposing vehicle's AC. On a success, you deal bludgeoning damage to the opposing vehicle, if its size category is equal to or less than yours. The damage is equals two rolls of a dice of a size equal to your car's hit dice size, plus your Dexterity modifier.

### Fighting on Vehicles

When fighting while in a vehicle, or fighting a creature on a vehicle, attack rolls are made with disadvantage and creatures have advantage on saving throws against any creature not moving at similar speed. If multiple vehicles are moving next to each other at a similar speed, the creatures in them act in combat as normal, as if they were not moving at all.

### Getting In and Out

You can enter or exit a vehicle by using half of your movement on your turn. If you are the driver, you can then use your action to have the vehicle begin moving. Otherwise, the vehicle begins moving at the start of your next turn.

If you wish to jump from one vehicle to another while both are moving, you'll need to perform a long jump, making a Strength (Athletics) check with the DC based on the distance and speed, in order to do so successfully.

### Crashes & Wiping Out

When a vehicle wipes out or crashes, the vehicle and all passengers take substantial damage.

When a vehicle wipes out, it takes bludgeoning damage equal to a roll of half of its total hit dice (rounded down) + its Constitution modifier, as does each creature in the vehicle unless they succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw, in which case they instead take half damage.

When a vehicle crashes, all of the above is the same, except the damage equals a roll of all of the vehicles total hit dice + its Constitution modifier, and the damage type changes to force for both the vehicle and the creatures inside, as the Infernal Engine of the vehicle explodes.

## Vehicle Properties

Vehicles use stat blocks, much like monsters. Here is a rundown of what everything means.

### Stats

When fighting while in a vehicle, or fighting a creature on a vehicle, attack rolls are made with disadvantage and creatures have advantage on saving throws against any creature not moving at similar speed. If multiple vehicles are moving next to each other at a similar speed, the creatures in them act in combat as normal, as if they were not moving at all.

- **Seats.** Indicates the number of total passengers a vehicle can comfortably hold, including the driver.  
- **Hit Points.** Indicates a car's health. When it drops to 0, the vehicle is destroyed. Vehicles cannot spend hit dice to recover their hit points, they must instead be repaired using [Runetech Tools](/📚%20-%20Information%20Compendium/items/runetech-tools-ccg.md).  
- **Ability Scores.** The stat blocks for vehicles don't include Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma. All vehicles have 0 in these stats and automatically fail any ability checks or saving throws that use these scores.  

### Speed

A vehicle indicates a speed in its stat block. This indicates the usual maximum speed it can move each turn, though it takes at least two rounds of movement for a vehicle to reach that speed. Here is a list of usual speeds for land vehicles per turn, depending on the environment:

**Slow (Tight Spaces).** 30 feet (3.5 mph)

**Average (Narrow Streets).** 300 feet (35 mph)

**Fast (Highways).** 500 feet (60 mph)

**Very Fast (Chase).** 700 feet (80 mph)

**Breakneck (Ludicrous).** 950 feet+ (100 mph+)

> [!note] Speed Isn't Everything
> 
> When two vehicles are in a race or in a pursuit, you should consider not only those vehicles' max speed, but the environment they are driving through. If they have to take many sharp turns or perform maneuvers through traffic, having a better driver is often much more valuable than having faster car.

## Vehicle Upgrades

Vehicles can be upgraded with a variety of additional parts that augment their properties. These upgrades are split into two categories—body upgrades and weapon upgrades, which can be found in the tables below. A vehicle can't have more total body or total weapon upgrades than it has seats. Each body upgrade can only be chosen once.

### Using Weapon Upgrades

Vehicle weapons can be operated by passengers in the vehicle, as long as they have proficiency in martial weapons. The provided vehicle weapons detail a specific weapon attack, and this attack can be made as an action, requiring the user to use both of their hands. Vehicle weapons use the operating character's Strength or Dexterity score (based on the weapon) as well as their proficiency bonus (PB) in their attack and damage rolls. If a character can make multiple attacks as a part of the attack action on their turn, they can make that many attacks when they use their action to attack with a vehicle weapon.
