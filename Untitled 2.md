## Running The Game

Now, to set expectations:



### Mechanics
- I have been for the better part of 9 months or so, working on a massive project of basically:
  - finding and incorporating any homebrew I like that I feel is a benefit to the game without creating issues
  - taking what works from different systems and translating that into 5e
  - creating my own personal homebrew rules or slightly tweaking existing rules to better suit the goals of my table
  - "building" a website (forking an existing one, and making lots of changes) with all of this incorporated
  - editing the website and adding features for better UX

culminating in what is basically a 5.1e or 5.75e (or whatever you wanna call it). A heavily modified version of the game that still uses base D&D as its core. Some of the errata includes:

- a total of ~30 NEW Classes, such as the:
  - Alchemist
  - Bender (yes, like Avatar)
  - Psion
  - Jaeger
  - Warlord and the Gunslinger
- Anywhere from 20-35 new subclasses for each of the base 13 classes
- New spells, weapons, feats, backgrounds, races, monsters/enemies, and such
- Large scale rebalance of the game like
  - Reworking certain spells that were outliers in either direction or outright removing some egregious examples
  - Reworking the base classes ranging from a small QOL change or buff to a heavy framework shift in some cases
  - Rebalancing certain conditions and effects, how equipment interacts with the player, resting mechanics
  - Adding new skills and buffing weaker ones, changing the Saving Throw system
  - Adding exploits/maneuvers for martials to rival the versatility and experience of spells for spellcasters
  - Adding more social/exploration options to character progression

And some more that I’m sure I’m forgetting. **If this makes absolutely no sense to you, great! You don’t have to worry about anything. Once I get everything finished some features may be seamless or as if they were apart of the base game, others will take a very minor explanation once you have context.** 

### Style

*GENERALLY*, I run games that focus more on the **Social** and **Combat** pillars of D&D (I’m trying to incorporate **Exploration** better, but we’ll see how that goes). I enjoy the grittiness and crunch of combat and tactics, so I will be doing my best to design encounters that reflect that. I’m hoping to be able to create meaningful choices both in and out of combat, where you’ll have to manage your resources and work together to overcome the obstacle. Obviously, this won’t be all the time or even *most* of the time, but that is what' I’ll be striving for. Which is to say:

**IF YOU DO NOT ENJOY COMBAT OR GRITTINESS OR MANAGING HEALING/COMBAT RESOURCES AT ALL, YOU WILL NOT ENJOY MY GAMES**.
